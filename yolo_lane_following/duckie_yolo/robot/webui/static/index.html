Calibration

        

        
Saved to . Do the steps in order. Redo the floor
          after re-doing the lens.

        
Needs a checkerboard with 7×5 inner corners and 31 mm squares:
          printable checkerboard (A4). Print at 100 %,
          check a square is 31 mm, glue it to cardboard so it stays flat.

  1. Press Start capture. The view switches to raw.
    2. 
          
  2. Hold the board in front of the camera and move it slowly: near and far, tilted, into all four corners.
            A view is saved whenever the board is found and has moved.
    3. 
          
  3. At 30 views (at least 8), press Calibrate lens.
    4. 
        

        

          
          
          
        

        
        

        

      


      

        
2 · Floor

        
        
x0: forward distance from the axle centre to the nearest row of inner corners
          (where black squares touch), in metres. y0: sideways position of the right-most column of inner corners;
          right is negative. Board centred in front of the robot → y0 = −0.093.

        

          
          
          
        

        

      


      

        
3 · Motors: power and driving straight

        
IMU: …

        
The robot_io running on the robot is older than this page and
          has no motor control yet. On the robot: git pull, then
          sudo systemctl restart duckie-robot-io.


        
a) Gyro

        
Robot on the floor, about 30 cm free around it. It stays still for 1.5 s (gyro offset), then
          turns left (counter-clockwise, seen from above) for half a second.

        

        

        

        
b) Start power

        
Motors stall when they get too little power. Each moving wheel gets at least this much.
          Find automatically pivots on one wheel at a time, slowly adding power until the gyro sees the
          robot move (robot on the floor).

        

          
          
        

        
        
        
        

        
c) Straight and accurate turns (PID)

        
The gyro measures how fast the robot really turns, and the PID corrects the wheels 100 times
          a second until it matches what was asked: 0 when driving straight, the model's turn rate in curves.

        
        
        
        
        

          
          
        

        

        
        
asked turn rate · measured
          by the gyro (rad/s, last 8 s, + = left)

        

          Tuning tips
          
    
            
  • Still drifts to one side slowly → raise Ki.
    • 
            
  • Weaves left-right quickly → lower Kp (or add a little Kd).
    • 
            
  • Corrects too slowly / overshoots turns → raise Kp a little.
    • 
            
  • Change one value at a time and run Drive straight again: aim for under ±5°.

Dataset guidelines

        
The model was trained in the simulator. A few hundred well-labelled frames from your track teach
          it what real tape, lighting and this camera look like.


        
1. Before recording

        
    
          
  • Calibrate the camera lens first (Calibrate tab). Frames are always saved raw too, so older
            sessions can be fixed with Re-correct.
    • 
          
  • Charge the battery; wipe the camera lens.
    • 
        


        
2. What to record

        
    
          
  • 300–500 frames per track / lighting setup. More variety beats more frames.
    • 
          
  • All of the track: straights, left and right curves, intersections. Drive both directions.
    • 
          
  • Mistakes and recoveries: drift towards an edge, point the robot ±30° off the lane, then steer back.
            A model that only sees perfect driving cannot recover.
    • 
          
  • Obstacles: duckies and other duckiebots in your lane and in the other lane, near and far.
    • 
          
  • Lighting: lights on and off, daylight, shadows across the road.
    • 
          
  • A few empty frames (floor or walls with no track), so it learns not to see lines everywhere.
    • 
          
  • Drive slowly (0.1–0.2 m/s): sharp frames. At 3 Hz and 0.2 m/s you get a frame every ~7 cm.
    • 
        


        
3. Red stop lines

        
The robot stops when a red line is about 22 cm ahead, waits 2 s, then drives straight across. Record it doing
          exactly that:

        
    
          
  1. Approach slowly with recording on, so the line is seen far, close and right in front.
    2. 
          
  2. Stop just before the line for a second or two.
    3. 
          
  3. Cross straight; keep recording through the middle of the intersection.
    4. 
          
  4. Every intersection, from every direction; some approaches off-centre or at an angle.
    5. 
          
  5. Also record red lines that belong to other lanes or are seen from the side.
    6. 
        


        
4. Avoid

        
    
          
  • Fast turns (motion blur), standing in one spot while recording (duplicate frames).
    • 
          
  • Hands, feet or cables in view, a dirty or covered lens.
    • 
        


        
5. Labelling

        
Label the corrected images (rect/): that is what the model sees. Use YOLO segmentation
          (polygons), with exactly these classes in this order:

        

          
idclasswhat

          
0yellow_laneeach dash of the dashed yellow centre line

          
1white_lanesolid white edge lines, including the white corners at intersections

          
2red_stopevery red stop line in view, also those of other lanes

          
3duckierubber ducks

          
4duckiebotother robots

        

        
    
          
  • Label every visible instance, including pieces cut off at the image edge.
    • 
          
  • Follow the tape edges tightly; leave the floor, reflections and glare unlabelled.
    • 
          
  • Never label red tape as white or yellow.
    • 
        


        
6. From recording to a better model

        
    
          
  1. Download the session zip (Datasets tab).
    2. 
          
  2. Pre-label it with the current model (on the laptop):
            
    python -m duckie_yolo.robot.autolabel_real --images <session>/rect \
    --weights models/best.pt --out data/real/labeled
    
            (or --segmenter color, sometimes better on clean tape; compare the previews).
    3. 
          
  3. Fix the masks in CVAT or Label Studio; export as YOLO segmentation into the same folder.
    4. 
          
  4. Fine-tune on simulator + real frames:
            
    python -m duckie_yolo.train --data data/sim_v1/dataset.yaml \
    --extra-train data/real/labeled/images --model models/best.pt \
    --epochs 30 --device cpu --name sim_real_v1
    5. 
          
  5. Upload runs/segment/sim_real_v1/weights/best.pt on the Model tab (rename it, e.g.
            real_v1.pt), build its TensorRT engine, check it in Model view before letting it drive.
    6. 
          
  6. Where it still fails, record more of exactly that situation and repeat.
    7.
